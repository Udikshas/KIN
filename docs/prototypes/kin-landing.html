How a call works

Two to four minutes, once a day, at the time your parent chooses.

  1. Talks

    Kin calls in the language or dialect they speak at home. The first call opens with a message you recorded, so they know who sent it.

  2. Checks

    Pain, sleep, appetite, mood and who visited. Kin compares each answer with what they said last week.

  3. Reminds

    Amma, did you take the white tablet? Then it confirms she did, instead of only sounding an alarm.

  4. Remembers

    For dementia, Kin answers the fortieth question as patiently as the first, and can reply with a voice note you recorded.

  5. Reports

    One card reaches the family each evening on WhatsApp or the Kin app. No dashboard to learn.

The conversation is the sensor

Kin compares today with your parent's own last 30 days.

Not with a population average. A quiet person stays quiet; Kin looks for the change.

What they say

Pain, sleep, appetite, mood, medicine, who came by.

How they say it

Pause length, speech rate, word finding, range of vocabulary.

Whether they answer

Three missed calls in a row is itself the first warning sign.

Dignity and privacy

Your parent decides what you see.

Summaries, never recordings

The family gets a short card. Audio is deleted after processing; no one reads a raw transcript.

They can pause Kin any time

Say “not today” and Kin calls tomorrow. Say “stop” and the calls stop.

When Kin isn't sure, it asks

If it didn't catch a word, Kin repeats back what it heard. Unclear moments go to a human for review instead of a guess.

A wellbeing check, not a doctor

Kin doesn't diagnose or prescribe. It helps the people who do arrive with a clearer picture.
